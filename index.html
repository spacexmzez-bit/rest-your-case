Field Manual: How to Play

Complete operational directives for the Rest Your Case tactical engine.

1. The Game Cycle

  • Phase 1 (Intake): You have limited turns to interview your client in holding. Ask precise questions to lock down their alibi. If you don't find the holes in their story, the DA will.
  • Phase 2 (Pre-Trial): Direct Investigator Diaz. This is where you spend AP to subpoena bank records, trace IP logs, or file motions to suppress tainted discovery.
  • Phase 3 (Trial): Cross-examine State witnesses. Use the `!` menu to object to improper DA questions in real-time. Introduce admitted exhibits to impeach lying witnesses.
  • Phase 4 (Verdict): Deliver a closing argument. The jury renders a verdict, and the "Truth Vault" in your Notebook decrypts to reveal what actually happened.

2. The Cockpit & Terminal

The Live Terminal uses a Split-Bench system.

  • Desktop: The Left Panel acts as an immutable ledger (tracking marked evidence and established facts). The Right Panel is the real-time dialogue stream.
  • Mobile: The cold-data panel is hidden to save vertical space. You can review all logged evidence and facts securely in the Notebook tab at any time.

Note: The input bar and objection button (`!`) are permanently pinned to the bottom of your screen. They will never scroll out of view.

3. Resource Economics

Action Points (AP)

AP represents your firm's time and budget. Issuing subpoenas, ordering forensic analysis, and filing formal motions cost AP. General conversation with your client or Partner is free.

Judicial Strikes (0/3)

The Judge tracks your conduct. Making frivolous arguments, pursuing badgering lines of questioning, or having your objections repeatedly overruled earns strikes. Max strikes = Mistrial/Contempt.

4. Command Line Syntax

While you primarily use natural language to talk, system directives use slashes:

/recap - Summarizes the trial so far (useful if you hit context limits).
/inspect [Ex. 1] - Demands granular forensic data or chain of custody on a marked exhibit.
/subpoena [Target] - Spends AP to legally compel phone records, bank statements, or server logs.

5. Senior Partner Drawer

Stuck? Click the floating Partner tab on the right edge of the screen.

This opens a secure, independent chat thread with your firm's senior partner. You can ask for legal advice, bounce case theories around, or ask if a specific objection applies. Consultations cost 0 AP and 0 Strikes.

6. Case Management

The Notebook tab is your private workspace.

  • Logging Discovery: If the AI mentions a piece of evidence, manually log it via `+ Manual Entry`.
  • Status Toggles: Cycle evidence status between Marked, Admitted, or Suppressed.
  • Wiping Data: To start a fresh trial, click `Delete Case` (located in the Terminal header or Notebook) and type `CONFIRM` to wipe local storage.

Legal Library

Compendium of Criminal Defense tactics and Federal Rules of Evidence.

1. Criminal Defense Standards

Burden of Proof

The defense does not have to prove innocence. The State must prove guilt beyond a reasonable doubt. Poke holes in their narrative rather than inventing your own.

Exclusionary Rule

Evidence obtained through a violation of the 4th Amendment (warrantless/illegal searches) is tainted. File a motion to suppress to exclude "fruits of the poisonous tree."

Brady Material

The prosecution is constitutionally required to turn over any exculpatory evidence (evidence favorable to the defendant). If they hide it, demand a mistrial.

2. Complete Objection Matrix

Use the `!` button in the Terminal to rapidly deploy these during cross-examination.

FRE 802: Hearsay

Trigger: A witness testifies about what someone else said out of court to prove it was true.

"My neighbor told me he saw the defendant do it."

Exceptions: Present Sense Impression, Excited Utterance, Admissions.

FRE 611(c): Leading (Direct)

Trigger: The prosecutor feeds the answer to their own friendly witness in the question.

"You saw him holding the bloody crowbar, didn't you?"

Tactics: Force the DA to ask open "Who, what, where" questions.

FRE 602: Speculation / Foundation

Trigger: The witness guesses about something they didn't personally perceive, or guesses someone else's state of mind.

"I think he was planning to sell the stolen files."

Tactics: Demand the DA lay a foundation showing personal knowledge.

FRE 404(b): Improper Character

Trigger: The State mentions prior bad acts or past crimes to prove the defendant acted in accordance with that trait.

"He's a known liar who cheated on his taxes five years ago."

Defense: Past crimes do not prove current guilt.

FRE 403: Unfair Prejudice

Trigger: The evidence's probative value is substantially outweighed by a danger of inflaming the jury's emotions.

Introducing highly gruesome, unnecessary photos of a victim.

Assumes Facts Not in Evidence

Trigger: The question embeds an unproven allegation as an accepted premise to trap the witness.

"When did you stop embezzling the company funds?"

3. Cross-Examination Tactics

  • Impeachment by Prior Statement: If a witness changes their story on the stand, confront them with their original police statement (found in your Phase 2 discovery). "Isn't it true you told Detective Vance..."
  • Financial Bias: Always ask cooperating witnesses or "experts" if they are being paid by the State for their testimony, or if they were offered a plea deal in exchange for testifying against your client.
  • Sensory Limitations: Attack the conditions of perception. "It was midnight, raining, and you were 100 feet away without your glasses, correct?"
