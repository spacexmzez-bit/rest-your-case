1. Constitutional & Procedural Safeguards

Foundational standards governing criminal prosecutions under the Bill of Rights.

Defense Prerogatives

Burden of Proof

Core Standard

The defense carries zero legal obligation to prove innocence. The prosecution bears the unwavering burden to prove every statutory element beyond a reasonable doubt.

Tactical Mandate: Focus on dismantling the coherence of the State's narrative rather than manufacturing complex alternative timelines.

Fourth Amendment: Search & Seizure

4th Amend.

Protects against unreasonable government searches. Evidence obtained without probable cause, a valid warrant, or an established exception constitutes Fruit of the Poisonous Tree.

Procedural Tool: File a formal Pre-Trial Motion to Suppress to excise tainted discovery from the jury docket.

Brady Material (Due Process)

14th Amend.

The prosecution is constitutionally mandated under Brady v. Maryland to surrender all exculpatory or impeachment evidence favorable to the defense upon receipt.

Remedy: Intentional withholding warrants immediate Judicial Sanctions, evidence exclusion, or dismissal with prejudice.

Fifth Amendment: Self-Incrimination

5th Amend.

Bars coerced confessions and protects the defendant's absolute right to remain silent. Custodial interrogations conducted absent voluntary Miranda waivers are inadmissible in the State's case-in-chief.

Trial Note: The prosecutor cannot comment on or invite the jury to draw negative inferences from a defendant's silence.

Sixth Amendment: Confrontation Clause

6th Amend.

Guarantees the accused the constitutional right to confront and cross-examine adverse witnesses face-to-face. Out-of-court testimonial statements cannot substitute for live cross-examination (Crawford v. Washington).

Strategic Deploy: Strike recorded police interviews of non-testifying co-defendants or absent accusers.

Evidentiary Chain of Custody

Forensic Rule

The State must establish an unbroken custodial chain accounting for every officer, technician, and vault keeper who possessed physical evidence from initial seizure to courtroom introduction.

Command Directive: Run `/inspect` on marked forensic exhibits to expose logging lapses or unsealed locker transfers.

2. Federal Rules of Evidence (FRE Matrix)

Statutory courtroom objection triggers. Deployable via the `!` button in the Live Terminal.

Active Objection Keys

FRE 801 / 802: Hearsay

High Frequency

Trigger: A witness recounts an oral or written statement made outside the courtroom by a third party, offered specifically to prove the factual truth of what was asserted.

State Rebuttals / Exceptions: Present Sense Impression (FRE 803(1)), Excited Utterance under stress (FRE 803(2)), Opposing Party Admission (FRE 801(d)(2)), or Regularly Kept Business Records (FRE 803(6)).

Defense Action: Force the prosecutor to articulate the exact statutory exception or strike the testimony.

FRE 611(c): Leading on Direct

Form Objection

Trigger: On direct examination of their own friendly witness, the prosecutor frames a question that suggests or embeds the desired factual answer.

Rule Dynamics: Leading is prohibited on direct examination except for introductory foundational background. Leading is explicitly permitted during defense cross-examination.

Defense Action: Compel the DA to ask neutral, open-ended interrogatories ("Who, what, when, where, describe").

FRE 602: Speculation / Lack of Foundation

Competency

Trigger: The witness testifies to matters beyond their direct first-hand sensory perception, guesses motives, or attempts to read another person's subjective state of mind.

Rule Dynamics: A witness may only testify to facts they personally observed through sight, hearing, smell, or physical presence.

Defense Action: "Objection, lack of foundation. The witness has shown no personal knowledge of the defendant's thoughts."

FRE 403: Unfair Prejudice vs. Probative Value

Exclusionary

Trigger: The proffered exhibit or testimony carries slight genuine evidentiary value, but serves overwhelmingly to inflame the passions, bias, or disgust of the jury.

Balancing Standard: Excluded if its probative weight is substantially outweighed by danger of unfair prejudice, confusing the issues, or misleading the panel.

Defense Action: Block inflammatory autopsy imagery or unrelated sensational conduct through bench side-bars.

FRE 404(b): Improper Character / Propensity

Character Rule

Trigger: The prosecution attempts to introduce previous arrests, convictions, or uncharged immoral deeds to argue the defendant has a criminal disposition and must be guilty here.

Exceptions (MIMIC): Prior bad acts may only be introduced to demonstrate Motive, Intent, Mistake absence, Identity, or Common Scheme.

Defense Action: Demand an immediate curative limiting instruction or move for mistrial if character prejudice is fatal.

FRE 401 / 402: Irrelevance

Relevance

Trigger: The question or evidence fails to make any consequential fact in dispute more or less probable than it would be without the evidence.

Rule Dynamics: Irrelevant evidence is strictly inadmissible. Tangential distractions introduced to waste court time must be shut down quickly.

Defense Action: "Objection, relevance. The witness's personal financial debt has no bearing on this traffic stop."

Assumes Facts Not in Evidence

Form Objection

Trigger: The examiner embeds an unproven, contested factual conclusion into the premise of the question (e.g., "When did you stop tampering with the server logs?").

Mechanism: Traps the witness into unintentionally ratifying an unproven crime element regardless of whether they answer affirmatively or negatively.

Defense Action: Object immediately before your client or witness attempts to answer the loaded compound premise.

FRE 701 / 702: Improper Expert Opinion

Witness Qualification

Trigger: A lay witness (such as an arresting officer or bystander) offers specialized scientific, medical, or ballistic conclusions without being qualified as an expert under Daubert standards.

Rule Dynamics: Lay witnesses are restricted to rational perceptions (speed, odor, emotional demeanor). Scientific causation requires qualified methodology.

Defense Action: Move to strike unqualified forensics and demand a voir dire examination of the expert's credentials.

3. Statutory Charge Grades & Crimes

Statutory elements required for conviction, grading thresholds, and common defense counter-theories.

Penal Code Compendium

Homicide: Murder in the First Degree

Class A Felony

Required Elements: (1) Unlawful killing of a human being, (2) With malice aforethought, (3) Perpetrated with premeditation and specific intent deliberation, OR committed in the perpetration of an enumerated felony.

Defense Battleground: Attacking premeditation. Proving the defendant had no pre-formed reflective design reduces the charge immediately to 2nd Degree or Manslaughter.

Key Vectors: Lack of planning evidence, impulsive physical struggle, intoxication negating specific intent.

Homicide: Murder in the Second Degree

Class B Felony

Required Elements: (1) Unlawful killing, (2) Committed with malice aforethought, but lacking premeditation, OR resulting from reckless conduct evidencing a depraved indifference to human life.

Defense Battleground: Mistaken identity, justifiable self-defense, or absence of depraved indifference.

Key Vectors: Proving reasonable perception of imminent physical threat to establish self-defense justification.

Manslaughter (Voluntary & Involuntary)

Class C Felony

Voluntary: Intentional homicide committed in the sudden heat of passion caused by adequate legal provocation without a cooling-off interval.

Involuntary: Unintentional killing resulting from criminal recklessness, gross negligence, or during the commission of an unlawful misdemeanor.

Key Vectors: Complete absence of gross deviation from standard of care; accident without criminal negligence.

Wire & Financial Fraud (18 U.S.C. § 1343)

Federal Felony

Required Elements: (1) Having devised or intended to devise a scheme to defraud, (2) False representations material to the transaction, (3) Use of interstate wire communications, (4) Specific fraudulent intent (mens rea).

Defense Affirmative: Good-faith business failure. An erroneous forecast or bankrupt venture does not constitute criminal intent.

Key Vectors: Subpoena internal compliance logs with Diaz to show client acted under professional legal advice.

Grand Larceny & Embezzlement

Property Felony

Required Elements: (1) Unlawful taking and carrying away of another's personal property, (2) Value exceeding the statutory threshold (e.g., $1,000+), (3) With the specific intent to permanently deprive the owner thereof.

Defense Battleground: Claim of right held in good faith; dispute regarding inflated asset valuation by the State.

Key Vectors: Contest whether value exceeds the grand larceny threshold; demonstrate lawful possessory title.

Contraband & Narcotics Distribution

Statutory Class A/B

Required Elements: (1) Knowing and unlawful possession or trafficking of a proscribed substance, (2) With actual knowledge of its illicit character, (3) Possession demonstrated as either actual or constructive.

Defense Battleground: Mere proximity is not possession. In multi-passenger vehicles or shared apartments, the State must prove conscious dominion and control.

Key Vectors: Challenge lack of fingerprints on packaging and attack the forensic drug testing chemistry chain.

Cyber Intrusion & Espionage (18 U.S.C. § 1030 / CFAA)

National Security

Required Elements: (1) Intentionally accessing a protected government or corporate computer without authorization or exceeding authorized access, (2) Obtaining sensitive or classified records, (3) Transmitting or communicating said data with intent to harm the United States or benefit a foreign principal.

Defense Battleground: IP address attribution does not prove human identity behind the keyboard. Subpoena router logs to expose open Wi-Fi relays, compromised credentials, or automated spoofing scripts.

Key Vectors: Prove lack of encryption bypass; establish client had explicit administrative clearance or shared credentials.

4. Cross-Examination Directives & Tactical Playbook

Techniques to impeach State witnesses and systematically generate reasonable doubt.

Courtroom Art

⚖ Prior Inconsistent Statements

If a witness deviates from their original statements in the Phase 2 discovery file, pin them down immediately: "You testified today that the jacket was black, correct? Isn't it true that on the night of the arrest, you stated to Det. Vance that the jacket was navy blue?"

Reference FRE 613 impeachment foundation rules.

⚖ Financial Bias & Plea Deals

Expose cooperator bias. Inquire whether the witness was offered dropped counts, reduced jail time, or immunity in exchange for their trial testimony. For paid expert witnesses, highlight their substantial consultation fees.

Never let an informant present themselves as an altruistic citizen.

⚖ Sensory Perception Limitations

Attack observation conditions: "It was 2:00 AM, raining heavily, you were standing over 90 feet away across a 4-lane intersection, and you were not wearing your corrective lenses, correct?"

Undermine certainty without calling the witness an intentional liar.
